This unit spans the closing section of Chapter 20 (Documents) and the opening of Chapter 21 (Images). Together they cover extracting intelligence from document metadata, locating documents and pastes online, accessing rental vehicle receipts, and performing image searches and reverse image analysis.

## Document Metadata with FOCA

FOCA (launched as FOCA.exe from its "bin/FOCAPro" folder) is a Windows application that accepts document files by drag-and-drop. After selecting "Extract all metadata" and "Analyze metadata" from the right-click menu, it organizes findings into categories such as Users, Emails, Software, and Computers. Most of FOCA's features no longer work, but the document metadata view and its user interface remain useful. The tradeoff versus ExifTool is that FOCA is easier to navigate but lacks a spreadsheet export. ExifTool produces a less attractive but more portable result. Both tools are worth knowing.

Real-world example: Dennis Lynn Rader (the BTK killer) sent a floppy disk to the Wichita Police Department containing a Word document. Metadata in that document showed the author name "Dennis" and links to a Lutheran church. OSINT searches on those two details led to his identification and arrest.

## Manual Metadata Extraction from Office Files

PowerPoint (and other Office) files may contain more metadata inside their structure than the standard metadata panel reveals. The method: rename the .pptx file to .zip, then decompress it. The archive contains all embedded images (each analyzable for its own metadata), slide text, and XML files including "app.xml", which records the application version (for example, AppVersion 16.0000 for Office 2016) and unique user identifiers. Comparing those identifiers across documents from different sources can confirm or rule out common authorship. This technique can also surface copyright infringement, as the chapter illustrates with a PPTX found via Google that contained Bazzell's own OSINT flowcharts claimed under another name.

## OCR for Image-Only PDFs

Free OCR (free-ocr.com) converts scanned PDFs that contain no selectable text into editable text documents. This is useful when a PDF was scanned poorly or deliberately locked against copying.

## Text Archives and Book Search

The Internet Archive (archive.org) holds large collections of text files and books. Limiting a search to text content uses the parameter `&sin=TXT`, for example:
`https://archive.org/search.php?query=inteltechniques&sin=TXT`

Google Books (books.google.com) has scanned most books from the past decade and provides a searchable index with digital previews. A direct query takes the form:
`https://www.google.com/search?tbm=bks&q=inteltechniques`

Z-Library (b-ok.ee/fulltext) indexes the text of millions of pirated e-books and allows full-text search without downloading. A query URL has the form:
`https://b-ok.ee/fulltext/michael bazzell/`
Results show the book title, author, and a text excerpt. The author notes this resource has investigative value for finding references to targets in publications, but explicitly advises against downloading any content because it likely violates law, policy, or ethics.

## Book Sales Data

Amazon's ASIN system allows lookup of sales-related details through amazon-asin.com. Given an ASIN (visible on the book's Amazon listing), the URL `https://amazon-asin.com/asincheck/?product_id=<ASIN>` returns estimated daily sales, estimated daily income, Amazon fees (listing fee, fulfillment fee, royalty percentage), listing quality rating, potential sales rating, and buyer keywords. The author notes results are not always accurate and should be treated as indicators, not facts.

## Rental Vehicle Records

Several major rental companies allow anyone to retrieve a receipt by supplying the renter's driver's license number and last name (or in some cases a credit card number). The author frames these as controversial techniques requiring individual judgment about appropriateness.

- Enterprise (enterprise.com): "Get a receipt" link at the bottom of every page. Requires driver's license number and last name. Returns six months to three years of rental history (the author's own test yielded two years). Each record includes dates, vehicle make and model, pickup location, total mileage, lessee name, and payment method.
- Hertz (hertz.com): Same "Get a receipt" link. Accepts driver's license number or credit card number with last name.
- Alamo (alamo.com): "Past Trips/Receipts" link. Identical process but requires the user to specify a date range (typically one year back to today).
- Thrifty (thrifty.com): Requires last name plus driver's license or credit card number.
- Dollar (dollar.com): Same requirements as Thrifty.

## IntelTechniques Documents and Pastes Tools

The downloadable "Documents.html" tool from IntelTechniques provides two search panels. The first queries by file type across Google, covering PDF, DOC, DOCX, XLS, XLSX, CSV, PPT, PPTX, KEYNOTE, TXT, RTF, XML, ODT, ODS, ODP, ODG, ZIP, RAR, 7Z, JPG, JPEG, PNG, MPG, MP4, MP3, and WAV. The second queries document hosting platforms including Google Docs, Google Drive, Microsoft Docs, Amazon AWS, CloudFront, SlideShare, Prezi, ISSUU, Scribd, PDF Drive, Wikileaks, Archive.org, Google Books, and Z-Library. A "Submit All" button in each panel opens every search simultaneously in separate tabs. A companion "Pastes" tool provides a Google custom search engine that queries all paste sites at once.

## Images (Chapter 21 opening)

Cameras on every smartphone have made photo uploads common on social networks. Images can reveal where a photo was taken, the make, model, and serial number of the camera used, original uncropped versions of photos, and other photos online taken with the same device.

## Standard Image Search

Google Images (images.google.com) is the recommended starting point because it has already indexed most major photo-sharing sites. A search on Google Images returns results from Flickr, Tumblr, and hundreds of other services simultaneously. Search Tools allow filtering by date, size, color, and type (photograph vs. line drawing). Bing Images (bing.com/images) offers the same filtering options and should not be skipped. On several occasions it has surfaced evidence not visible in Google results.

## Reverse Image Search

Reverse image search provides an image as the query instead of text. Uses include finding other websites where a target used the same photo, discovering alias profiles that use a real photo, identifying copied images, and in some services estimating the sex and age of subjects.

All five services should be checked for each investigation. Direct URL submission is possible at each, which allows automation. Using the example image at `https://inteltechniques.com/img/EP2.png`, the direct URLs are:

- Google: `https://www.google.com/searchbyimage?site=search&sa=X&image_url=https://inteltechniques.com/img/EP2.png`
- Bing: `https://www.bing.com/images/search?view=detailv2&iss=sbi&q=imgurl:https://inteltechniques.com/img/EP2.png`
- TinEye: `http://www.tineye.com/search/?url=https://inteltechniques.com/img/EP2.png`
- Yandex: `https://yandex.com/images/search?rpt=imageview&url=https://inteltechniques.com/img/EP2.png`
- Baidu: `https://graph.baidu.com/upload?image=https%3A%2F%2Finteltechniques.com%2Fimg%2FEP2.png`

Google (images.google.com) introduced reverse image search in 2011. To use it, right-click an image in Firefox and select "view image" (Chrome: "open image in new tab"; Internet Explorer: "properties") to get the direct image URL, then paste that into the Google Images reverse search field. Adding context such as a name or employer to the search field after submission improves accuracy for profile photos. Clicking a result image and choosing "Search by image" also triggers a reverse search.

Bing launched its "Visual Search" in 2014 via a camera icon in the search field. It returns fewer results than Google in most cases, though it occasionally finds matches Google misses.

TinEye (tineye.com) focuses on exact duplicate images. Results are typically fewer than Google's. Upload from disk or enter a URL.

Yandex Images (yandex.ru/images) began allowing image uploads in 2015. As of 2020 it began producing accurate results not visible on Google, Bing, or TinEye. The author considers Yandex the best reverse image search option currently available.

Baidu Images (image.baidu.com) is a Chinese-language-only service. It offers reverse image search by URL or upload, but in the author's experience it fails more often than it works.

The author advises never submitting contraband or classified images to any of these services. Submitting child pornography to a search engine is legally distribution of that material. Classified images cannot be submitted due to case restrictions.

## Cropped Reverse Image Searching

Since roughly 2018, Google and Bing have returned fewer reverse image results, likely due to personalization filtering. A technique to counter this: crop the image to show only the specific target before submission. This works on Google and Bing but is most effective on Yandex. The author demonstrated this with a Twitter image where the full photo produced no useful matches, but a crop isolating the target returned multiple matches on Yandex.

## Specialized Reverse Image Services

Karma Decay (karmadecay.com) restricts results to Reddit only. Originally built to detect reposts for downvoting, it is useful for locating every copy of an image across Reddit. Accepts a URL or file upload. Direct URL format: `https://karmadecay.com/search?q=<image_url>`

Root About (rootabout.com) searches only the Internet Archive and Open Library. Results are typically published photos and book covers. Does not support direct URL submission.

Wolfram Image Identification Project (imageidentify.com) does not match images across the web. Instead it analyzes the content of an image and returns a description, such as the make, model, and year of a vehicle, or a translation of text in the image. Images can be uploaded or dragged from another browser tab.

Pictriev (pictriev.com) analyzes faces in a photo and attempts to locate additional images of the same person. It also estimates the sex and age of the subject. Results are strongest for public figures but it functions on lesser-known subjects as well.

## Twitter Images

Early Twitter required third-party photo hosts for image attachments. Twitter now hosts photos natively, but third-party hosting (including Instagram) remains common. After any Twitter search, a "Photos" filter in the results menu narrows results to image posts.

## Real-World Applications Cited

- BTK Killer (Dennis Lynn Rader): Word document metadata on a floppy disk revealed the author name "Dennis" and a Lutheran church link, leading to arrest.
- Cemetery vandalism (2011): A reverse image search of a damage photo returned a suspect's blog post showing him at the scene, leading to arrest and community service.
- Hotel image theft: A reverse image search identified dozens of companies using a client's licensed hotel photos without authorization, leading to civil litigation.
- Human trafficking case: A reverse image search from a prostitution ad located all of a trafficking victim's other ads, revealing regional work areas, a cellular number tied to her pimp, and approximate dates of activity.
