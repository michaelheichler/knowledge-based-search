## People Search Engines (Continued from Chapter 17)

This unit continues the people search engines chapter with sites beyond the main tier.

**PeopleSearchNow** (`peoplesearchnow.com`) is useful when a target has opted out of related services. Direct URL: `https://www.peoplesearchnow.com/person/first-last`.

**WebMii** (`webmii.com`) focuses on social network data. It rarely surfaces home addresses or phone numbers but has returned images not found on other search engines. Direct URL: `http://webmii.com/people?n=%22First%20Last%22`.

**Social Searcher** (`social-searcher.com`) focuses on social media presence and post mentions. Direct URL: `https://www.social-searcher.com/search-users/?q6=First+Last`.

**TruthFinder** (`truthfinder.com`) offers data similar to other services but buries it behind fake progress meters that push a standard search past 14 minutes. Use the direct URL to bypass them: `https://www.truthfinder.com/results/?firstName=First&lastName=Last&state=ALL`.

**PeopleByName** (`peoplebyname.com`) misleadingly accepts only a phone number on its standard interface, but a specific URL structure returns all profiles for a given name: `http://www.peoplebyname.com/people/LastName/FirstName`.

**White Pages** (`whitepages.com`) performs reverse name or address search and often includes known residents and neighbors. Direct URL: `https://www.whitepages.com/name/First-Last`.

**ClustrMaps** (`clustrmaps.com`) offers a direct URL query: `https://clustrmaps.com/persons/First-Last`.

**Find People Search** (`findpeoplesearch.com`) shares a database with many other services, but its records are not being updated. Stale data can be useful because it may show a previous address that more current services no longer display. No direct URL. Manual search only.

**Public Records** (`publicrecords.directory`) queries voter registration databases, campaign contribution disclosures, and vehicle registration data. Results are inconsistent. No direct URL. Manual search only.

**RocketReach** (`rocketreach.co`) focuses on business details likely scraped from LinkedIn and online resumes. The site requires a login to browse, but a Google search of the form `site:rocketreach.co "target name"` returns viewable profiles. Profile data includes location, employment history, education, partial email addresses, and partial phone numbers. Full details require a free account.

## IntelTechniques Names Tool

Bazzell built a local tool (available in the downloadable archive) that simultaneously launches separate tabs for every people-search service. A user enters the first and last name once and clicks "Submit All." Middle names are intentionally omitted because many sites do not store them and including one would cause missed results. The tool covers TruePeopleSearch, FastPeopleSearch, Nuwber, XLEK, FamilyTreeNow, Intelius, Radaris, CyberBackgroundChecks, Spytox, SearchPeople, JohnDoe, Spokeo, AdvancedBackgroundChecks, Yasni, Zabasearch, PeopleSearchNow, WebMii, SocialSearcher, TruthFinder, PeopleByName, WhitePages, ThatsThem, and ClustrMaps, along with tabs for Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, LinkedIn, email addresses, usernames, telephone numbers, maps, documents, pastes, images, and videos.

## Supplementary Name-Based Resources

**How Many Of Me** (`howmanyofme.com`) uses census data to report how many people in the United States share a given name. A low count means search results are more likely to apply to the target. A high count means results will be diluted with other people. Bazzell has also used the site to identify rare names suitable for cover identities.

**Classmates** (`classmates.com`) requires a free account, which may contain fictitious information. After logging in, a search by name and school returns enrollment years and provides access to scanned yearbooks. Yearbooks must be browsed manually page by page and typically contain information not available through any other search.

**Ripoff Report** (`ripoffreport.com`) is a user-submitted complaint database covering businesses and individuals who deal with the public. It is useful for investigations into fraud or repeated bad service.

## Rebate Tracking Portals

Retail rebate tracking pages expose purchase history and, in some cases, home address and phone number with no authentication required.

**Menards** rebates are tracked at `https://rebateinternational.com/RebateInternational/tracking.do`. Entering a first initial, last name, street number, and ZIP code returns purchased items, purchase dates, rebate amounts, and partial check numbers. The site generates a static URL in the format `https://rebateinternational.com/RebateInternational/trackResults/INITIAL/LASTNAME/STREETNUMBER/ZIPCODE` (example: `.../M/Smith/1212/77089`). No login is required, making this URL pattern open to brute-force scraping. Results also carry pretext value since they confirm specific purchases a target made.

**Lowe's** rebate tracking at `https://lowes-rebates.com/en-us/RebateStatus` accepts a cell phone number or mailing address. A known cell number can therefore be converted to a name and address, or vice versa. Shopping history is also returned.

Other retailers including Micro Center, Budweiser, Miller, Kohl's, and auto parts stores offer similar portals. To find them, search Google for `intitle:"rebate center"`, `"track+my+rebate"`, or `"track+your+rebate"`.

## Utility Company Portals

If a target's phone number and ZIP code are known, some ISP and utility troubleshooting pages will return a full name and address. The CenturyLink example uses the internet troubleshooting page at `https://www.centurylink.com/home/help/internet/test-your-internet-at-the-network-interface.html`. Clicking "Open Troubleshooter" presents a form that accepts phone number and ZIP code. Submitting it returns the customer's full name, address, and phone number. Other local utilities may offer equivalent portals.

## Resume Searching

Resumes are high-value targets because they often contain cell numbers and personal email addresses that a person would never post to social media. Searches on Google, Bing, and Yandex using the following patterns locate resumes hosted on websites and cloud document services:

```
"Target Name" "Resume"
"Target Name" "Curriculum Vitae"
"Target Name" "CV"
```

Each of these can be combined with `filetype:doc`, `filetype:pdf`, or `site:docs.google.com`. Some resume hosting services store resume images rather than text to block scraping, but Google's OCR indexes the image content. Running the same queries on Google Images (`images.google.com`) therefore surfaces image-only resumes.

**CV Maker** (`cvmkr.com`) hosts over five million resumes. There is no native search, but a Google site search of the form `site:cvmkr.com "target name"` returns a PDF containing full contact information including email, phone, and home address. If the PDF is missing from the result, appending `?pdf=1` to the profile URL always returns the full resume view (example: `https://cvmkr.com/PROFILEID?pdf=1`). Google indexes the PDFs, so phone numbers and emails can also be searched using the site operator.

**Indeed** (`resumes.indeed.com`) has a large resume database. The "Find Candidates" search returns work history, education, and location. Contact information is usually redacted.

## Gift and Baby Registries

Wedding and baby registries are posted online and frequently remain accessible long after the event. They contain details that targets do not consider sensitive but which are investigatively valuable.

Partner name is disclosed by any wedding registry entry. The target's partner is often less aware of the investigation and continues to post information online.

Maiden name can be recovered by switching a wedding registry search to display past weddings instead of upcoming ones. This opens up further searches of yearbooks, criminal records, and prior addresses.

Wedding date and state are often enough to request a marriage certificate, which may include full names of parents, witnesses, and the officiant. The anniversary date is also useful as a social engineering element because many people use it as a security question answer.

Ceremony details published on registry sites such as The Knot include how the couple met and who is in the wedding party, identifying the target's closest associates.

Registry items reveal travel plans, gear preferences, honeymoon destination, restaurant reservations, and consumer habits.

The Knot (`theknot.com/registry/couplesearch`) requires both first and last name, but entering `**` as the first name returns all entries matching only the last name. The Bump (`registry.thebump.com/babyregistrysearch`) only searches upcoming births, but past registries can be found via Google: `site:registry.thebump.com "Target Name"`.

## Deceased Individuals

## Voter Registration

People who have unlisted phone numbers may still appear in public voter registration databases. A directory of all 50 states' online county databases is at `www.blackbookonline.info/USA-Voter-Records.aspx`. Records typically include full name and full address and can be sorted by name or by address.

## Unclaimed Property

Every US state maintains an unclaimed property website. Common categories include dormant bank accounts, uncashed dividends, unclaimed refunds, and leftover utility balances. A last-name search typically returns full name, home address, property type, and amount owed. The central access point for all state sites is `https://unclaimed.org`. Results can also serve as pretext for a phone call.

## Address Google Searches

To find all indexed content tied to a specific address, use separate quoted strings for the street address and the city: `"1234 Main" "CityName" ST`. Putting the full address into a single quoted string causes results to be missed when address components appear non-adjacently on a page. This search commonly surfaces home sale listings that include interior photographs.

## Telephone Numbers (Chapter 18 Introduction)

When starting from a telephone number, Bazzell describes three sequential phases: (1) identify the number type and provider, (2) identify subscriber information such as name and address, and (3) locate online content connected to the number.

### Carrier Identification

Ten years before the book was written, investigators used number porting sites to find the carrier, then served court orders on that specific carrier. Those services have since disappeared or moved behind paywalls. Five years before the book, VOIP numbers became dominant, appearing as "Broadband" or "Internet" rather than a named carrier.

**Free Carrier Lookup** (`freecarrierlookup.com`) is the most stable current option. It accepts domestic or international numbers and returns country, service type (mobile, landline, or VOIP), and the specific provider name. It is the best tool for resolving VOIP numbers to identifiable companies.

Confirmed provider mappings from Bazzell's own testing:

| Known provider | Type displayed | Name returned |
|---|---|---|
| Verizon | Mobile | Verizon Wireless |
| AT&T | Mobile | AT&T Wireless |
| Google Voice | VOIP | Google/Level 3 |
| Sudo | VOIP | Twilio/Level 3 (SMS-Sybase) (MMS-SVR) |
| Blur | Landline | Twilio/Level 3 Communications |
| TextNow | VOIP | Enflick/Bandwidth.com (SVR) |
| On/Off | VOIP | Peerless Network |

Backup options are **Carrier Lookup** (`carrierlookup.com`) and **Phone Carrier Check** (`phonecarriercheck.com`). If both disappear, a Google search for `"carrier lookup"` will surface current alternatives.
