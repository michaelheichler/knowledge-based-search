This unit covers the tail end of the email address chapter and the opening of the username chapter. It presents a collection of services, query methods, and investigative techniques for extracting intelligence from email addresses and usernames.

## Email Address Investigation

### Breach and Leak Services

Running every target email through breach services is a standard step. A positive result confirms the address is real and has been in use. No result in any service suggests the address is new or a one-time "burner" account. Recovered password details may be accurate or wrong. Attempting to log in to any account using recovered credentials is computer intrusion and is never appropriate. These resources support passive intelligence gathering only.

**s-check** queries the same database maintained by Avast but does not add the email to Avast marketing campaigns and does not notify the account owner. It confirms whether the address appeared in a breach without naming which breach.

**CitOday Leaks** (breach.myosint.com) is a collection of over 23,000 data breaches leaked from the former CitOday website, containing hundreds of millions of exposed email addresses and passwords. Sites such as Have I Been Pwned indexed this data, but none of those services reveal which specific databases include a given address. CitOday Leaks may return results unique from other services.

**Scylla** (scylla.sh) was, at time of writing, listed as offline with a return planned. When available it presents unredacted passwords for billions of accounts at no cost. Queries must be submitted in a specific format. Placing raw terms in the search field returns nothing. The required syntax is:

    email:test@test.com
    password:password12345
    name:inteltechniques

These can also be submitted directly as URLs:

    https://scylla.sh/search?q=email:test@test.com
    https://scylla.sh/search?q=password:password123
    https://scylla.sh/search?q=name:bazzell

A key technique enabled by Scylla is password recycling detection. If a breach record shows a unique password for a target email, that same password can be queried to find other email addresses using it, which likely belong to the same person. When scylla.sh is offline, alternatives are https://scylla.so/api or http://44.235.17.188/api.

Other breach services discussed in the chapter include Have I Been Pwned (haveibeenpwned.com), Dehashed (dehashed.com, paid tier shows unredacted passwords), and Spycloud (paid tier for passwords).

### Verification and Profile Discovery

**Hunter Verifier** (hunter.io/email-verifier) validates an email address and shows internet links that contained it, including links to pages that have since been removed and are no longer indexed by Google. This makes it useful for documenting historical presence on forums or sites.

**OCCRP** (data.occrp.org), described as a global archive for investigative reporting, allows keyword searches that can be filtered by email address. A keyword search returns documents, and clicking the "Email" filter reveals all email addresses embedded in those documents. Querying an email address directly shows documents associated with it.

**Search My Bio** (searchmy.bio), noted earlier in the book as an Instagram search tool, also accepts email addresses. If a person included their email in their Instagram biography and that profile was indexed, a search here may find it. Searching "@gmail.com" returns nearly 500,000 profiles that include a Gmail address, making it a fast path to an Instagram account when the email is known.

**Spytox** (spytox.com) is a white-pages style lookup that may return a name, city, and telephone number. Paid options are not considered worth the cost.

**XLEK** (xlek.com) contains email addresses in its data but provides no email search field. A Google dork serves as a workaround:

    site:xlek.com "mike@gmail.com"

This pattern applies to any people-search site that displays emails but lacks a dedicated search field.

**That's Them** (thatsthem.com) rarely returns results, but occasionally produces full name, address, phone, and email. It is included in a standard sweep.

**Public Email Records** (publicemailrecords.com) is believed to be populated from the River City Media data leak. Results include full name and address.

**Search People Free / Cyber Background Checks** (searchpeoplefree.com, cyberbackgroundchecks.com) draws from marketing database leaks and often returns full name, age, current and previous addresses, phone numbers, family members, and business associations.

**Many Contacts** (manycontacts.com/cn/mail-check) is a premium service offering a free individual email lookup. It identifies linked social network accounts including LinkedIn, Twitter, Facebook, Foursquare, Instagram, and Flickr, with direct hyperlinks to each profile. It is recommended as one of the first searches when a target's primary email is known. Common VPN providers may be blocked.

### People Data Labs

**People Data Labs** (peopledatalabs.com) offers 1,000 free queries after creating an account. It accepts masked email addresses such as those from 33mail, Anonaddy, and Simple Login. Because no traditional search interface is provided, queries are submitted as URLs:

    https://api.peopledatalabs.com/v5/person/enrich?pretty=true&api_key=YOUR_KEY&email=target@example.com

Results are returned in JSON format and can include full name, birth year, LinkedIn URL and ID, Facebook URL and ID, Twitter URL, mobile phone number, additional email addresses, and education. The IntelTechniques email tool accommodates this service but requires the investigator to replace a placeholder with their own API key in the tool's source code.

### ProtonMail Account Dating

ProtonMail is a widely used encrypted email service. Because many criminal investigations involve ProtonMail accounts, determining when an account was created is often useful. A recent creation date may indicate a burner account.

**Method 1 (requires a free ProtonMail account, more reliable):**
1. Create a new contact in ProtonMail and enter the target email address.
2. Open the contact, click "Email Settings."
3. Click "Show advanced PGP settings."

The displayed public key creation date is the account creation date in most cases. The exception is when the account owner has generated new security keys, in which case the key date reflects that action rather than account creation. This is rare.

**Method 2 (no login required, but susceptible to false positives on VPN):**

First, confirm the account exists:

    https://api.protonmail.ch/pks/lookup?op=get&search=notmyemail@protonmail.com

If the browser prompts to download a file named pubkey.asc, the address exists. A "No Key Found" message means it does not.

If the address exists, retrieve the timestamp:

    https://api.protonmail.ch/pks/lookup?op=index&search=notmyemail@protonmail.com

The response includes a line ending in a string of digits (for example, 1623879788) that is a Unix epoch timestamp. Converting it at https://www.unixtimestamp.com/index.php gives the account creation date. False positives can occur when connected to a VPN server that has abused this process or when queries are submitted in rapid succession.

### Domain Connections via Reverse WHOIS

Every domain registration includes an associated email address. Reverse WHOIS services accept a target email and return domain names registered with it, including expired domains. Services discussed:

- **Whoxy** (whoxy.com/reverse-whois): returns full name, mailing address, phone number, owned domain names, and registrar and host details.
- **Whoisology** (whoisology.com): returns similar data.
- **DomainBigData** (domainbigdata.com): returns similar data.
- **AnalyzeID** (analyzeid.com): returns Amazon Affiliate ID, AdSense Affiliate ID, and owned domain names, but not full contact information.

### ScamSearch

**ScamSearch** (scamsearch.io) allows an email address to be queried against reported online scam databases. A wildcard URL search is broader than a standard query and may catch misspellings or similar accounts:

    https://scamsearch.io/searchadvanced?_emailwild=email&search=protonmail.com

Replace the domain with the target's domain or partial address. The service is most reliable for email and Bitcoin queries.

### Email Provider Identification via MX Records

A business or custom-domain email address does not reveal its hosting provider by name. The domain's MX (mail exchanger) record does. This information may be needed for a court order, to document an investigation, or to link entities using the same small provider. Changing email providers over time can indicate intent to conceal evidence.

To look up the MX record, go to MX Toolbox (mxtoolbox.com) and enter the domain portion of the address (for example, phonelosers.org). The result gives a hostname and IP address identifying the email provider.

### Imitation Technique (Passive Account Existence Check)

Attempting to register an account at a service using the target email address reveals whether the target already holds an account there. If the service reports the address is "already in use," the account exists. This technique should be applied only to the first registration screen and never submitted or completed.

Services to test include apple.com/account, signup.live.com (Windows/Microsoft), Yahoo, Gmail, Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, and local cable, power, and water utility companies. Identifying that a target uses a specific local cable provider can significantly narrow the geographic area of residence.

### IntelTechniques Email Addresses Tool

The IntelTechniques Email Addresses Tool consolidates all of the above resources into a single interface with "Populate All" and "Submit All" buttons. It covers search engines (Google, Bing, Yandex), social platforms (Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, LinkedIn), breach services (Trumail, Emailrep, Gravatar, HIBP, Dehashed, Spycloud, CitOday, Cybernews, PSBDMP, IntelX, LeakedSource), verification tools (HunterVerify, OCCRP, SearchMyBio, SpyTox, ThatsThem, ProtonMail), domain tools (DomainData, Whoisology, AnalyzeID), and business or government data (PeopleDataLabs, ScamSearch). The PeopleDataLabs entry requires the investigator's own API key to be inserted into the tool's source code.

## Username Investigation

A known username from one platform is often reused across many others. When a target's email is known, the local part (the portion before the "@") is a likely candidate username on other services. The email address itself can also suggest alternate addresses on other providers: if mpulido007@gmail.com is known, mpulido007@yahoo.com, mpulido007@hotmail.com, and mpulido007@aol.com are worth testing.

### Username Search Services

**KnowEm** (knowem.com) checks a username against the top 25 social networks on the main page. An extended search covers over 500 networks organized by category. Direct URLs:

    https://knowem.com/checkusernames.php?u=inteltechniques
    https://knowem.com/checksocialnames.php?u=inteltechniques

When a site shows the word "available" stricken through, a profile exists under that username. When the word "available" appears normally (orange and underlined), no profile was found.

**Check Usernames** (checkusernames.com) searches roughly one-third of the networks covered by KnowEm and links directly to found profiles.

**Name Checkr** (namecheckr.com) performs a similar search more quickly and provides live hyperlinks to identified accounts.

**User Search** (usersearch.org) returns only actual profile results and searches over 100 popular websites. It is the slowest of these tools. Direct URL queries bypass the manual navigation requirement:

    https://usersearch.org/results_normal.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_advanced.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_advanced1.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_advanced2.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_advanced4.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_advanced5.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_advanced6.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_advanced7.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_dating.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_forums.php?URL_username=inteltechniques
    https://usersearch.org/results_crypto.php?URL_username=inteltechniques

**NameVine** (namevine.com) provides a live partial-match search. As a partial username is typed, it immediately shows registered accounts across the top ten social networks (Twitter, Facebook, Pinterest, YouTube, Instagram, Tumblr, WordPress, Blogger, GitHub) and checks for a matching .com domain. This is useful when the exact username variation is uncertain. Direct URL:

    https://namevine.com/#/inteltechniques

**Social Searcher** (social-searcher.com) differs from the others. It returns social network posts that mention the username rather than accounts owned by the target. Direct URL:

    https://www.social-searcher.com/search-users/?ntw=&q6=inteltechniques

**Whats My Name** (whatsmyname.app) replicates many features of the above services and adds the ability to export results to clipboard, XLSX, CSV, or PDF. It checks sites including GitHub, Gravatar, Internet Archive, Medium, Reddit, Spotify, and Twitter.

### Manual Direct Profile Checks

Testing the username directly against the URLs of common platforms is a fast complement to the automated tools:

    https://twitter.com/TARGET
    https://facebook.com/TARGET
    https://instagram.com/TARGET
    https://www.tiktok.com/@TARGET
    https://tinder.com/@TARGET
    https://TARGET.tumblr.com

### Breach Lookup by Username

Dehashed supports direct username queries:

    https://dehashed.com/search?query="inteltechniques"

Have I Been Pwned and the other breach services listed in the email section can also be searched by username where the service permits.
