## Chapter 22 (Closing): Video Tools and Live Streams

### Generic Video Downloaders

When a video service is not covered by dedicated tools, two websites can attempt to download or convert embedded video content without any software configuration: KeepvidPro (keepvid.pro) and ClipConverter (clipconverter.cc).

### Live Video Streams

Live streaming services allow a person to turn a smartphone into a real-time broadcast device. The phone's camera streams over cellular or Wi-Fi to the host service, which distributes the feed to simultaneous viewers with roughly a five-second delay. Each platform has a keyword search for finding active streams. Services are listed in order of investigative preference: YouTube (youtube.com/live), LiveStream (livestream.com), Twitch (twitch.com), LiveU (liveu.tv), YouNow (younow.com), and UScreen (uscrccn.tv). The Video Stream Tool in the IntelTechniques toolkit provides a more stable capture option, especially when kept updated via automated scripts.

During a large violent protest, Bazzell used live streams to identify new disturbances, locate victims needing medical attention, and spot fires set by arsonists, information that directly shaped the response of law enforcement, fire, and EMS.

### Additional Video Utilities

PeteyVid (peteyvid.com) is a multi-platform video search engine. It searches video hosts beyond Google's index. In one example, a unique keyword returned no Google results but PeteyVid found a relevant video on bitchute.com.

Listen Notes (listennotes.com) is an audio podcast search engine, not a video tool, but is presented in this chapter as a media service. It indexes millions of podcasts by keyword. An email address search for "privacypodcast@protonmail.com" returned one result from old episode show notes. As podcast volume grows and audio-to-text technology improves, this technique becomes increasingly useful.

Anilyzer (anilyzer.com) provides frame-by-frame video analysis for YouTube and Vimeo content. Enter the video URL, select the provider, and click "Watch Video" to step through individual frames or adjust playback speed. It is useful for quickly examining a handful of frames in surveillance footage without full video-editing software.

The IntelTechniques Videos Tool (Figure 22.03) consolidates all video investigation functions into one interface, including YouTube metadata retrieval, thumbnail extraction, reverse image search of thumbnails, age bypass, restrictions checking, downloading, archiving, and keyword searches across Google Videos, Bing, Yandex, YouTube, Twitter, Facebook, Reddit, and TikTok.

## Chapter 23: Domain Name Investigation

A domain is the base address of a website (for example, inteltechniques.com). The "www" prefix and any subdirectory paths are not part of the domain itself. Every domain should be researched across current registration, historical registration, archived content, and visual history.

### Whois Data

Every domain requires registrant, administrative, and technical contact information to be filed with the registrar, which passes it to ICANN, making it publicly available. This record, called Whois data, contains a full name, business name, physical address, telephone number, and email address. Although ICANN requires accuracy, this is rarely enforced, so criminals frequently supply false information. A second complication is private registration, where the owner pays a proxy service to appear in Whois in their place. If a domain shows a private registration entry such as "WhoisGuard Protected," the true owner is masked.

### Current Registration: ViewDNS.info

ViewDNS.info is the primary current-registration tool, preferred for its ten-year reliability and its support for direct URL queries, which allows integration into custom tools. Available lookups include:

- Whois (viewdns.info/whois): returns registrant details, creation date, and update date.
- Reverse IP (viewdns.info/reverseip): translates a domain to its IP address and lists all other domains hosted on that server. If the co-hosted domains share a common theme, the server is dedicated. Many unrelated domains from around the world indicate shared hosting, which weakens attribution.
- Reverse Whois (viewdns.info/reversewhois): finds other domains registered by the same entity. Fails if private registration is present.
- Port Scanner (viewdns.info/portscan): checks common ports for open services. Ports 80 (HTTP) and 443 (HTTPS) are standard. An open port 21 indicates an FTP server accessible at ftp.[domain].
- IP History (viewdns.info/iphistory): lists past IP addresses associated with the domain, including which organization owned each IP and when it was last observed.
- DNS Report (viewdns.info/dnsreport): full DNS configuration including mail server details.

When ViewDNS blocks a VPN connection, who.is is an alternative Whois tool.

URL templates for all ViewDNS queries follow a consistent pattern with the target domain as a parameter, for example:
`https://viewdns.info/whois/?domain=cnn.com`

### Historical Registration: Bypassing Private Whois

When current Whois is masked, the strategy is to find records from a period before privacy was added. Several services store historical snapshots.

**Whoxy (whoxy.com)** is the top recommendation for free historical domain records. A direct URL query (https://www.whoxy.com/[domain]) reveals past registrant names, email addresses used during registration, and the dates of each record. A "Domains" link aggregates all domains a person has ever registered under that identity. The search box also accepts email addresses, names, and keywords to discover what domains a target owns without knowing the domain names in advance. Paid tiers offer bulk query options. Whoxy exposed more private registrations than any other free service during Bazzell's 2019 investigations.

**Whoisology (whoisology.com)** requires a free account. It shows cross-reference counts alongside each Whois field: the number of additional domains that share the same registrant name, email address, street, phone number, or ZIP code. Clicking any count lists all matching domains. Historical archives record every change to a domain's registration, including telephone numbers that have since been removed. Free accounts are limited to one historical record and three searches per 24 hours, which is why Whoxy is the default first choice. A paid Whoisology subscription provides the most comprehensive data for large-scale domain investigations but is expensive.

**Domain Big Data (domainbigdata.com)** is fully free. It offers less cross-referencing than Whoisology but does provide historical Whois snapshots and can link email addresses to additional domains. A search of a privately registered domain confirmed private status for the current record while surfacing a historical entry from 2013 that revealed the registrant's real name.

**Security Trails (securitytrails.com) and WhoisXMLAPI (whoisxmlapi.com)** offer limited free trial access to historical domain data. Full access requires API integration.

### Wayback Machine Whois Archives (Free Bypass for Private Registration)

The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) may have captured Whois lookup pages before a domain added privacy protection. Querying those archived pages reveals historical registrant data including real names, addresses, phone numbers, and email addresses. The technique involves constructing a Wayback Machine URL pointing to a Whois service page for the target domain. The earliest available capture yields the most historical exposure. Five Whois services with known Wayback Machine coverage are Who.is, Domain Tools, Whoxy, Domain Big Data, and Whoisology. This method requires no paid subscription.

Example (replace cnn.com with the target):
`https://web.archive.org/web/http://www.who.is/whois/cnn.com/`

In a demonstration with phonelosers.org, the current record showed WhoisGuard protection. A Wayback Machine query of the same Who.is URL returned a 2017 capture with the registrant's real name, address, phone, and email, and the earliest capture from 2010 contained two additional phone numbers.

### Historical Content Archives (Site Content Over Time)

When a domain has changed or gone offline, archived copies of the site content itself are the resource.

**Internet Archive Wayback Machine:** Direct URL format is `http://web.archive.org/web/*/inteltechniques.com`. Site owners can request exclusion, and that request is honored here. When exclusion is in effect, use the alternatives below.

**Archive Today (archive.is / archive.fo / archive.md):** Ignores all deletion requests. Captures are accessible at `https://archive.is/*.inteltechniques.com`. Bazzell's own site, excluded from Wayback, had 183 captures here dating to 2013.

**Mementoweb Time Travel (mementoweb.org):** Aggregates archives from multiple third-party providers. Query format: `http://timetravel.mementoweb.org/list/19991212110000/http://inteltechniques.com`

**Library of Congress Web Archives (webarchive.loc.gov):** Searchable by domain. Query format: `https://webarchive.loc.gov/all/*/http://inteltechniques.com`

**Portuguese Web Archive (arquivo.pt):** Found 12 historical archives of inteltechniques.com dating to 2017. Query format: `https://arquivo.pt/page/search?hitsPerPage=100&query=site%3Ainteltechniques.com`

### Historical Screen Captures

Visual documentation of a target website serves three goals: the current appearance, historical versions, and future change monitoring.

**Search engine cache:** Google holds the most recent cache of most pages. Direct URL: `https://webcache.googleusercontent.com/search?q=cache:inteltechniques.com`. Bing and Yandex also hold caches.

**Website Informer (website.informer.com):** Provides a historical screen capture alongside standard site statistics.

**URLScan (urlscan.io):** Captures are often unique compared to other services. Query URL: `https://urlscan.io/domain/inteltechniques.com`

**Easy Counter (easycounter.com):** Another source of historical captures, similar in coverage to Website Informer.

**Spyse (spyse.com):** High-resolution, relatively current captures.

**DomainIQ (domainiq.com/snapshot_history):** The preferred screen capture tool. Results include the capture date, the IP address at time of capture, and the hosting provider. In one investigation, a suspect deleted content from a domain before the case began. The Wayback Machine, Google, Bing, and Yandex had no record of the site. DomainIQ had a single screen capture made months earlier that clearly showed the content needed as evidence.

**Domain Apps (dmns.app):** Provides a historical screenshot via direct URL: `https://files.dmns.app/screenshots/inteltechniques.com.jpg`
