This chapter (labeled Chapter 31 in the source) covers three topics for OSINT practitioners: how to handle illegal digital content encountered during investigations, the ethical principles governing intelligence gathering, and how to build a career or organizational program in open source intelligence.

## Handling Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM)

OSINT work can expose investigators to illegal content. The most critical category is Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM). The term "child pornography" is still used in some laws but is considered dated. CSAM is the preferred term in notes and incident documentation.

The chapter provides a seven-step protocol for investigators who encounter CSAM without a preexisting policy.

1. **Do not capture it.** Avoid saving images or video for two reasons. Copying the material creates additional harm to victims, and simple possession is a felony in many jurisdictions. Record only URLs and non-graphic identifying information. Investigators trained for child exploitation cases follow their defined procedures instead.
2. **Notify a supervisor.** Immediate notification documents that the investigator acted in good faith and ensures all procedural requirements are met.
3. **Report to law enforcement.** Reporting obligations vary by jurisdiction, but the ethical responsibility is universal. Even if the local agency cannot fully investigate, the report creates a documentation trail.
4. **Report to NCMEC.** Submit a report to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children via missingkids.org/gethelpnow/cybertipline and save a screenshot of the submission.
5. **Report to the platform, with timing judgment.** Notify the hosting platform, but only after notifying law enforcement, to give investigators time to request evidence preservation. If the platform may be involved in criminal activity, do not tip them early. Law enforcement typically serves a preservation letter before content is removed.
6. **Document the incident.** Record date, time, investigator name, supervisor, URLs, and a description sufficient to convey why the material may be contraband. Do not reproduce any media.
7. **Wellness.** Exposure to CSAM causes lasting emotional harm even for experienced investigators. Consulting a professional counselor after exposure is appropriate and encouraged, whether the encounter was intentional or accidental.

### Sample CSAM Policy Framework

The chapter includes a model policy with eight sections. Key provisions:

- **Definitions** (Section 1.1): CSAM covers any still, video, or audio depicting sexually explicit conduct involving a minor. A minor is any individual whose appearance would lead a reasonable person to believe they are under 18.
- **Reporting responsibilities** (Section 1.2): All instances are reported to legal authorities. Supervisors are notified immediately and must ensure material is not further propagated.
- **Post-incident procedures** (Section 1.3): Equipment exposed to the material is examined by technology personnel and sanitized before further use.
- **Documentation** (Section 1.4): Personnel do not copy or reproduce any CSAM. Only location information (internet addresses, IP addresses, domain names) is captured.
- **Exceptions** (Section 1.5): Units with child exploitation investigation as a primary duty require specific exemption from their commander and operate under their own specialized procedures.
- **Training** (Section 1.6): All employees review and acknowledge the policy. Units with online investigations as a primary duty complete additional training.
- **Revision history** and **Appendix** sections round out the template.

The author recommends having any drafted policy reviewed by in-house legal counsel. For students and volunteer investigators, the recommendation is to ask organizers of crowd-sourced or charity investigations for written direction before participating.

## Ethics

Privacy is the core ethical consideration in OSINT. Deception and scope of investigation are secondary concerns that arise depending on the nature of the work.

### Scope of Investigation

Investigators must define scope early. Two contrasting scenarios illustrate the range.

- A threat assessment of a public official's account where the posts, on review, contain no credible threat. Once that determination is made, no further digging into the person's private life is justified.
- A tip that an IP address is transmitting child exploitation material, where the IP turns out to be a Tor exit node. Even though the resident may be uninvolved, the severity of potential harm justifies exhausting all available public data. The seriousness of the crime determines how broad a scope of intrusion is warranted.

### Publicly Accessible but Unintentionally Exposed Data

Some data is legally accessible but was made public by mistake, such as an Elasticsearch database with no authentication requirement. The author's position is that intent matters. Using such data for defensive vulnerability assessments or serious crime investigations is justifiable. Using it for minor matters or low-stakes intelligence is not.

### Intent and Justification

Every OSINT engagement requires an articulable reason. Standard justifications include investigating a reported crime, background checks on prospective employees, vendor vetting, and vulnerability assessments for organizations. A useful rule: any OSINT work that provides personal gratification is a red flag. Examples of improper use include investigating ex-partners, people encountered in public, or anyone outside a legitimate operational need.

### Doxing

Doxing is defined as using online research to profile an individual and then publishing that information publicly with intent to create fear or embarrassment. It constitutes cyber-harassment and can rise to criminal liability.

### Deception

Deception is any behavior that causes another person to believe something untrue. Two forms appear regularly in OSINT work.

- **Covert accounts.** Using fictional or anonymous accounts violates the terms of service of most platforms but is treated as necessary for effective social media research. If the investigator's intent is sound, the justification is articulated, and the account is used only within the scope of the investigation, the deception is considered proportionate.
- **Infiltration ("friending").** Joining groups or interacting directly with targets is more invasive than passive observation because it changes group dynamics and directly affects individuals. It has legitimate application, for example joining criminal forums to deanonymize child predators, but requires explicit justification before any interaction.

The chapter closes the ethics section by calling ongoing training in both OSINT and privacy law an ethical obligation. Sloppy casework harms everyone the investigator is meant to serve.

## Professional Development

### Formal Education

No single educational background is required. Successful practitioners come from diverse fields, military backgrounds, and internal organizational training. Communication skills, both written and verbal, are described as indispensable because almost every assignment produces a written report. A resume functions as an intelligence report representing the investigator's own capabilities and should be concise, organized, and error-free.

### Building Experience

Experience is the most valuable and hardest-to-acquire qualification. Paths the author identifies:

- **Military service.** Provides initial intelligence training, real-world experience, potential language exposure, and pay at the same time.
- **Law enforcement or government.** Many agencies have OSINT units, but placement typically requires working through the ranks over several years.
- **Private sector.** Departments such as loss prevention offer lower entry requirements while building investigative skills, serving as a stepping stone.
- **Creating a program.** Proposing an OSINT function within a current employer that lacks one is viable if framed with concrete demonstrations and success stories.
- **Volunteering.** Unpaid internships with teams doing this work, contingent on passing a background check, frequently lead to full-time positions.
- **Starting a business independently.** Not recommended for early-career practitioners. Surrounding oneself with experienced peers produces better learning outcomes.

Early-career attitudes that matter most: eagerness to learn, willingness to do menial tasks, and patience. Entry-level positions outside the intelligence function still provide valuable foundational experience.

### Building an Organizational OSINT Program

A proposal for management should address:

- Industry benchmarking showing that specialized OSINT teams are standard and improve efficiency.
- How increased situational awareness reduces liability and supports field operators in critical incidents.
- A proposed timeline and success metrics.
- Estimated costs for equipment, training, and staffing, with note that surplus or previously seized hardware is a viable low-cost option.
- A sample policy and standard operating procedures.
- A communications, documentation, and approval plan.
- Location options if the team needs to be centralized.

Management concern centers on risk and cost. The pitch should position the organization as behind current practice with an opportunity to lead. The author advises expecting management modifications to the plan and controlling expectations about how long a fully capable unit takes to develop.

### OSINT-Specific Training

Self-directed (autodidactic) learning is viable on a near-zero budget because the skill of online research is the same tool used to find better techniques. Working through problems independently strengthens investigative problem-solving capacity. A browser, this book, and determination are described as a sufficient foundation.

Live courses and bootcamps offer the fastest learning for those with budget, combining demonstration with hands-on labs. Online video training sits between live instruction and self-study, offering demonstration without labs. The author recommends researching instructors and previously released content, including community discussions of courses, before committing to any training program. IntelTechniques.net is cited as the author's own training resource offering both video courses and live sessions.
