This chapter (labeled Chapter Twenty-Seven in the book) covers how to extract investigative value from online mapping services. It walks through each major service, provides static URL patterns for programmatic querying, and describes a custom tool that automates querying all of them from a single set of GPS coordinates.

## Core concept

The starting point for any location query is a pair of GPS coordinates (latitude, longitude). Throughout the chapter, 41.947242 (latitude) and -87.65673 (longitude) are used as the working example. Once you have coordinates, you can construct direct URLs to every service described, which makes the process repeatable and automatable.

## Google Maps

Google Maps (maps.google.com) accepts full addresses, partial addresses, GPS coordinates, or business names in its search bar. Key view modes:

- **Standard map view** and **satellite view** are the baseline. Satellite view looks almost straight down.
- **Globe view** (accessed via "More" under layers) offers imagery from a different capture date. Enabling 3D and the rotation icon shifts the perspective 45 degrees per click, revealing angled views.
- **Street View** is opened by dragging the orange figure icon to a location. Navigation works by clicking, dragging, or scrolling. Double-clicking moves to the view from that new point.
- **Historic Street View**, available since late 2014, lets you select from all stored captures at a location. Choose "See more dates" to access the timeline. Earlier captures may show vehicles or structures no longer present.

Distance measurement: right-click a starting point, choose "Measure distance," and click to build a path. GPS coordinates appear by clicking any point (or right-clicking and choosing "What's here").

Static URL patterns (substituting coordinates into the template):

- Standard map: `https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=map&center=LAT,LNG&zoom=18`
- Terrain: add `&basemap=terrain`
- Satellite: add `&basemap=satellite`
- Street View directional views use `map_action=pano` with `heading` (0 = north, 90 = east, 180 = south, 270 = west), `pitch` (0 = horizontal, -90 = straight down), and `fov` (field of view).

## Bing Maps

Bing Maps (bing.com/maps) provides map, satellite, angled "Bird's Eye View," and street view. Its imagery is independent of Google and frequently shows different content for the same location. A side-by-side comparison often reveals which service has better imagery for a given target.

URL patterns use `cp=LAT~LNG` and `lvl=20` for maximum zoom. Directional satellite views add `sty=o` and `dir=0/90/180/270`. Street views add `style=x` with the same directional parameters.

## Zoom Earth

Zoom Earth (zoom.earth) aggregates imagery from NASA, Bing, and ArcGIS. The ArcGIS layer is occasionally more recent than Google or Bing. Its full-screen mode eliminates interface chrome, making it suitable for presentations or documentation. URL format: `https://zoom.earth/#view=LAT,LNG,20z`.

## Here

Here (wego.here.com) originated as a Nokia mapping product with distinct imagery but now largely mirrors Bing. Unique images appear occasionally, so it is included in the toolkit. URL format: `https://wego.here.com/?map=LAT,LNG,20,satellite`.

## Yandex

Yandex (yandex.com/maps) satellite quality is generally poor for U.S. locations but better for international targets. URL format: `https://yandex.com/maps/?l=sat&ll=LNG%2CLAT&z=20` (note longitude comes first in the URL parameter).

## Apple Maps

Apple released a web browser version of Apple Maps in 2024 at beta.maps.apple.com. URL parameters: `t=m` (standard), `t=k` (satellite), `t=h` (hybrid), `t=r` (road). The zoom parameter (`z=1`) was unreliable at time of writing.

## Dual Maps

Dual Maps (data.mashedworld.com/dualmaps/map.htm) places Google and Bing satellite views side by side in a single browser window, enabling quick visual comparison of which service holds better imagery for a location.

## Historic imagery

When current imagery is not sufficient, historical sources extend the timeline:

- **Historic Aerials** (historicaerials.com): satellite and aerial images going back several decades. Quality degrades for older images, but the coverage can span twenty or more years for a single location.
- **World Imagery Wayback** (livingatlas.arcgis.com/wayback): preferred over Historic Aerials due to higher quality and more captures. The chapter shows four images of the same location from 2014 through 2017 as an example. Static URL includes an `active` parameter and `mapCenter`.
- **Old Maps Online** (oldmapsonline.org): road maps only (no satellite imagery), useful for tracing historical street names or determining when a road was constructed.

## Crowd-sourced street views

Standard Google and Bing street views blur most faces and license plates. Two crowd-sourced services offer views that may bypass these redactions:

**Mapillary** (mapillary.com/app): colored lines on the map indicate user-submitted GPS-tagged imagery, typically uploaded from dashcam or phone recordings. Clicking a line reveals the images in the lower screen. The uploader's username and capture date appear in the lower left corner. License plates in these images are blurred inconsistently. When a plate is far away and harder to read, the blur algorithm may not trigger. The chapter describes recovering a partially obscured plate by cropping the image, inverting it in Photoshop, reducing brightness to 0%, and raising contrast to 100%. URL format: `https://www.mapillary.com/app/?lat=LAT&lng=LNG&z=18`. A real case from 2020 is cited in which Mapillary revealed an unknown vehicle parked outside a missing person's residence that led investigators to the victim.

**Karta** (kartaview.org, formerly Open Street Cam): functions identically to Mapillary but holds distinct imagery. Karta also exposes uploader profiles showing their username, mapping history, total images, and travel routes. URL format: `https://kartaview.org/map/@LAT,LNG,18z`.

## Weather and environmental satellites

**Land Viewer** (eos.com/landviewer): weather-satellite imagery limited to city-scale zoom. Four active satellites collect current images. Five inactive satellites hold historical data back to 1982. Primary use case is documenting weather conditions (clouds, rain, clear sky) at a crime scene on a specific date.

**Landsat Look** (landsatlook.usgs.gov/explore): similar to Land Viewer, with additional historical captures dating back to 1972.

## Property tools

**Acre Value** (acrevalue.com): uses Mapbox for aerial imagery and overlays property boundary lines. A free account does not directly display owner information, but the data is accessible by opening the browser Inspector, switching to the Network tab, clicking the parcel of interest, and reading the `xhr` response from `https://www.acrevalue.com/map/api/plot/?id=<ID>`. The JSON response includes fields such as `owner`, `county_fips`, `apn`, `acreage`, `slope`, and `fema`.

**Satellites.pro** (satellites.pro): an unofficial third-party site that serves Apple Maps aerial imagery. URL format: `https://satellites.pro/USA_map#LAT,LNG,18`.

## Real estate sites

Zillow, Trulia, and Realtor.com frequently hold interior photographs of homes that were listed for sale. Interior images can reveal personal belongings, interests, and family details about a subject. Static URL structures exist for each service using coordinate-based bounding boxes and are used in the automated tool.

## Shadow analysis for date and time estimation

Satellite images contain shadows that can approximate the capture date and time. The tool used is **Shade Map** (shademap.app). The method: search the target location, then drag the month and time sliders until the shadow direction and length on Shade Map match the shadows visible in the satellite image. The chapter works through an example using a Wrigley Field satellite image in which shadows fell directly east-west with no north-south angle, indicating a capture near the spring equinox. Adjusting the time slider produced a match at approximately 7:28 am Central time in late March. The chapter recommends documenting a range (such as February through April, 7:00 to 8:00 am) rather than an exact time, since the method is approximate. The same technique applies to any photograph posted to social media when the general location and season are known.

## Custom annotation

**Scribble Maps** (scribblemaps.com): allows creation of custom annotated maps by adding shapes, lines, text, and images over a standard, satellite, or hybrid base map. Intended for producing court exhibits, client deliverables, or operational planning materials. The finished map can be printed, saved, or exported.

**Free Map Tools** (freemaptools.com): companion service with advanced options including radius mapping around a point of interest.

## IntelTechniques Maps Tool

The custom tool at inteltechniques.com/tools/Location.html automates the entire workflow. Enter a physical address in the first field to retrieve GPS coordinates, then paste the coordinates into the second section and click "Populate All." Clicking each query button opens a new tab with results from every service described in the chapter. The browser must have pop-ups enabled. The chapter includes thumbnail results (Figures 27.09 through 27.36) from searching Wrigley Field in Chicago, showing satellite, globe, bird's-eye, street view, and crowd-sourced results across all providers in a single session.
