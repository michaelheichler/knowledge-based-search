## Overview

This short section opener introduces Part I of the book, titled "OSINT Preparation." It explains why investigators must configure their computing environment before conducting any online research, previews the technical topics that follow in the section's chapters, and sets expectations for readers at all skill levels.

## The core argument

Earlier editions of this book opened directly with search techniques. This edition reverses that order deliberately. The author argues that jumping into an investigation without first securing the computing environment is reactive thinking. Investigators who browse suspect websites, search social platforms, or examine hacker content on unprotected machines are exposing themselves to contamination and risk. A prepared, clean environment must come first, just as a DNA forensics lab must be properly configured before any evidence is processed.

The argument is grounded in direct observation. During a multi-day OSINT training course, the author watched police officers search Facebook on patrol vehicle laptops, private investigators run old versions of Windows while viewing suspect content, and cybersecurity professionals visit hacker websites without antivirus software, script blockers, or a VPN. The author admits having done the same things earlier in his career.

## Terminology: host and guest

The section introduces two terms used throughout the following chapters. The "host" is the investigator's existing desktop or laptop computer. The "guest" is any operating system running virtually on top of it. This host/guest distinction is foundational for everything in Section I.

## What Section I covers

The chapters in this section cover:

- Verifying and securing the host computer
- Configuring virtual machines for individual investigations
- Embedding Linux applications into Windows and Mac host environments
- Customizing OSINT software for ongoing availability
- Building custom search tools to automate queries
- Preparing a virtual Android environment for mobile investigations
- Cloning the complete environment so that a corrupted or compromised setup can be restored immediately

## What this section is not

Section I is not a comprehensive computer security guide. The author's intent is to propose the most beneficial strategies that protect investigators from the majority of problems, not to address every possible vulnerability.

## Guidance for readers

Readers are encouraged to skip technical sections they find difficult and return to them later, or to move directly to Section II (online OSINT techniques) or Section III (breach data) and treat Section I as a reference they can return to when ready. The author explicitly does not want technical difficulty to block readers from the parts of the book most relevant to their work.

The section closes with an invitation to read critically and form independent opinions, including disagreement with the author's choices.
