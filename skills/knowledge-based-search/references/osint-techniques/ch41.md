title: Government and Business Records
source: OSINT Techniques (Bazzell, Edison, 11th ed.)

This chapter covers publicly accessible government and business records in the United States, explaining what data is available online, where to find it, and how to query it. The central claim is that more government and business information is online than most people realize, though there is no uniform standard: each county, state, and jurisdiction organizes and exposes its data differently, so investigators must adapt their approach to each target location.

## County and Court Records

Black Book Online (blackbookonline.info/USA-Counties.aspx) aggregates links to county public record databases organized by state and county. A sample search for Madison County, Illinois surfaces coroner reports, recorded documents, unclaimed property, delinquent tax sale records, registered lobbyists, government expenditures, public employee salaries, voter registration verification, crime maps, building permits, foreclosed properties, and more, each linking directly to the original government source.

For court records, Black Book Online (blackbookonline.info/USA-County-Court-Records.aspx) similarly drills down to county-level court data. St. Clair County, Illinois serves as an example with its full civil and criminal court records searchable by last name, returning full name, date of birth, physical identifiers, case history, fines, pending appearances, and dismissed charges. The Madison County example exposes circuit court dockets, traffic citations, crash reports, police blotter, DUI dockets, and jail inmate search. Some rural areas are not yet online. Alternatives include Public Records Online (publicrecords.onlinesearches.com) and Netronline (publicrecords.netronline.com), the latter described as receiving more frequent updates.

PACER (pacer.gov) is the federal Public Access to Court Electronic Records system, covering U.S. district courts, courts of appeals, and bankruptcy courts. It holds over 800 million documents. Cost is $0.10 per page, capped at $3.00 per document. Fees are waived if quarterly usage stays under $15, and account creation is free.

RECAP (courtlistener.com/recap) is a Firefox and Chrome browser extension that works alongside PACER. Before a user pays for a document, it checks whether a free copy exists at the Internet Archive. If the user purchases the document, RECAP automatically uploads a copy to the Internet Archive's PACER database. A standalone search page exists at the address above.

UniCourt (unicourt.com) provides a large court document database but limits guests to three searches before prompting for a paid membership. Clearing browser cache and switching to a new VPN IP address appears to reset this limit. Because case summaries are publicly available, Google indexes most of them, so the query `site:unicourt.com "target name"` returns results linking directly to case pages. Free case pages show case number, filing date, jurisdiction, judge, courthouse, county, case status, defendant and plaintiff names, and docket entries with dates and actions. Full documents require a subscription.

Judy Records (judyrecords.com) offers free court record search with no stated business model and accepts traditional search operators including quoted name searches. The site was briefly shut down due to legal challenge over the breadth of its access and has since reopened with most functionality intact.

Trellis (trellis.law) provides court records for a subset of indexed states. Multi-state URL queries can be constructed by appending state parameters, for example: `https://trellis.law/cases/bazzell?state=az&state=ca&state=ct&state=de&state=fl&state=ga&state=il&state=ma&state=nv&state=nj&state=ny&state=oh&state=pa&state=ri&state=tx&state=wa`. A free account is required for full docket information.

## FOIA Records

FOIA Search (foia.gov/search.html) indexes government websites using Bing and surfaces records not always retrievable through Google. A static query URL format is `https://search.foia.gov/search?affiliate=foia.gov&query=osint`. MuckRock (muckrock.com) files FOIA requests on behalf of users and publishes all government responses publicly, with a query URL format of `https://www.muckrock.com/foi/list/?q=inteltechniques`.

## Business Records

Open Corporates (opencorporates.com) is described as the best overall business lookup tool. It indexes all 50 U.S. states plus dozens of countries, scraping data directly from government sources. Records typically include corporate officers' names, addresses, and contact details. It also preserves historical data that may no longer appear at the original source. Search supports business name, entity registration number, or officer name. An advanced address search requires a free account. URL query formats: `https://opencorporates.com/companies?q=inteltechniques` and `https://opencorporates.com/officers?q=bazzell`. This tool is visited every time a business name appears in an investigation, or when a target is likely associated with an organization.

Dun and Bradstreet (dnb.com) offers a business search but many companies do not participate.

AIHIT (aihitdata.com) uses AI to populate and update business records. Its "More Fields" option accepts email address, telephone number, or individual name to find associated businesses. URL formats include `https://www.aihitdata.com/search/companies?k=email@gmail.com`, with `?t=Michael Bazzell` for names and `?c=inteltechniques` for company names.

OCCRP Aleph (aleph.occrp.org) is a global archive of research material for investigative reporting, containing business records, transcripts, and data sets filterable by email address, phone number, name, and file type. Queries are free but results may be limited without a free account. URL format: `https://aleph.occrp.org/search?q=michael%20bazzell`.

Public Accountability (publicaccountability.org) accepts any name or term and returns government licenses, campaign contributions, federal grants, federal loan details, and government employee data.

Open Payrolls (openpayrolls.com) contains nearly 85 million salary records from over 14,800 employers including federal agencies, states, counties, cities, universities, and K-12 schools. Query URL format: `https://openpayrolls.com/search/michael-bazzell`.

EINTaxID (eintaxid.com) attempts to surface tax ID (EIN) numbers for large companies.

Little Sis (littlesis.org) maps relationships among powerful people and organizations, including donations, political support, and board memberships. Query URL: `https://littlesis.org/search?q=michael bazzell`.

## Identity and Death Records

SSN Verify (ssn-verify.com) validates whether a social security number is legitimate and returns the issuing state, year issued, assignment confirmation, and estimated age range. It does not return personal information tied to the number.

The Social Security Death Index (genealogybank.com/explore/ssdi/all) is a public index of death records requiring only first and last name, returning birth year, death year, state of last residence, and state of SSN issue.

Legacy (legacy.com/search) indexes obituaries and memorials from approximately 80 percent of all U.S. online newspapers. Results can identify family members and locations.

## Assets

Black Book Online's Asset Locator (blackbookonline.info/assetsearch.aspx) is described as the most comprehensive list of sources for searching real estate, judgments, bankruptcies, tax liens, and unclaimed funds. Selecting an asset type and state generates a page of direct links to relevant databases for that state, which often includes public employee salaries, vehicle registrations, and property tax records.

## Voter Registration Records

The 2016 and 2020 elections drew attention to voter registration data being used in campaign strategies. Regardless of that controversy, much of the data is publicly available online. Voter Records (voterrecords.com) and VoterRef (voteref.com) provide searchable voter registration data. Results include full name, home address, mailing address, gender, party affiliation, age, and relatives. States with active databases include Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Michigan, Nevada, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Rhode Island, Utah, Washington, and others. NCSL maintains a list of state availability at `https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists`.

## Vehicle Records

Vehicle registration and licensing data is not fully public, but a significant portion is available online.

The Department of Transportation VPIC tool (vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov) provides free VIN lookups from manufacturer data, returning year, make, model, trim level, style, manufacturing country, and weight. Additional VIN decoders include Vin Decoderz, FaxVin, Check That VIN, Vehicle History, Search Quarry, and VinCheck. Vin Decoderz, listed first among those alternatives, will often display estimated mileage based on service records.

The National Insurance Crime Bureau (nicb.org/vincheck) offers VINCheck Theft Records (vehicles reported stolen) and VINCheck Total Loss Records (salvaged vehicles). Standard VIN decoders do not provide this information.

Cycle VIN (cyclevin.com) handles motorcycle VINs, which may not work in standard decoders due to character count differences. Free use confirms year and make. Full title and mileage information costs $25.

License plate lookups can be attempted at Auto Check, Records Finder, CarFax, Search Quarry, Free Background Search, Carvana, VinCheck, Vehicle History, and Find By Plate. Results vary by state. California plates frequently return accurate data while states such as South Dakota and Montana restrict registration sharing. Results typically return a VIN rather than an owner name.

Kelley Blue Book (kbb.com/instant-cash-offer) is presented as a useful workaround. Entering a vehicle's license plate in the trade-in quote form returns year, make, and model. This worked for a test on a South Dakota plate "MRBIG" and a Wyoming plate, both states known to protect registration data.

O'Reilly Auto Parts (oreillyauto.com) accepts a license plate and state in its parts-lookup "Garage" feature and returns vehicle year, make, model, and engine specifications. A test of California plate "HACKER" returned "2007 GMC Sierra 1500 SLE, V8,6.0, 5967,364,Electronic, SFI,GAS, FI,MFI." Pairing this with a VIN lookup on VinCheck produced the VIN 3GTEK13Y87G527460.

Progressive (progressive.com) offers an insurance quote flow that, when given a zip code and physical address, returns all vehicles registered at that address including year, make, and model. Any other data entered in the quote form can be arbitrary.

## Marine and Aircraft

Marine Traffic (marinetraffic.com) provides a real-time interactive map of global ship and boat locations with vessel name, speed, collection time, and destination. Boat Info World (boatinfoworld.com) accepts a boat name and returns owner, registered address, hull ID, hailing port, Lloyd's Registry Number, call sign, Coast Guard Vessel ID, service type, build year, ship builder, hull shape, propulsion type, length, and gross tons.

Commercial aircraft constantly broadcast their location through automated reporting systems, and tail numbers function like license plates. Plane Finder (planefinder.net) displays a global real-time map of aircraft in flight, with carrier, flight number, departure, destination, speed, and altitude on hover. Historical ownership records for aircraft are available through Black Book Online's aviation page (blackbookonline.info/Aviation-Public-Records.aspx), which links to databases for aircraft N-number and ownership search, airline certificates, airport profiles, certified pilots, cockpit voice recorder data, flight trackers, and military aviation crash reports.

## Campaign Contributions

All U.S. political campaign contributions are public record. Open Secrets (opensecrets.org) and Melissa Data (lookups.melissa.com/home/) both allow name-based searches returning occupation, contribution recipient, amount, contribution type, and a link to the official filing. Filtering by zip code, occupation, and year is available after an initial search. Melissa Data also supports zip code queries to retrieve all donations for a given year. State-specific portals often contain unique data. Massachusetts is cited as an example with its portal at `https://m.ocpf.us/reports/searchitems`.

## Selective Service and Driver's License Numbers

Selective Service Verification (sss.gov/verify) requires last name, social security number, and date of birth, and returns full name, selective service number, and date of registration.

High Programmer (highprogrammer.com/cgi-bin/uniqueid) automates derivation of driver's license numbers from name, sex, and date of birth for states that use a predictable algorithm. States covered include Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, New Hampshire, New York, Washington, and Wisconsin.

## Credit Card BIN Lookup

BinDB (bindb.com/bin-database.html) accepts the first six digits of any credit card number, the Bank Identification Number (BIN), and returns brand, issuing bank, card type, card level, country of issuance, bank website, and customer care line. A case example from homicide investigation illustrates the utility: a debit card found at a restaurant was run through BinDB to identify the issuing bank, a call to the bank's automated system using the card number and the victim's zip code returned 30 days of transaction history, an ATM withdrawal on the day of the killing was identified, and ATM video then showed a passenger in the victim's vehicle who became a suspect.

## Criminal Records

Family Watch Dog (familywatchdog.us) searches registered sex offenders by address or last name, returning a photograph and offense details.

Federal prison records are searchable at bop.gov/inmateloc by first and last name. Each state maintains its own prisoner database, reachable by searching "Inmate locator" plus the state name in Google. In most states, records of subjects still on probation or parole remain in public view after release.

VINELink (vinelink.com) is a victim notification portal that, after selecting a state, allows searches for an offender's custody status, court dates, sex offender registry status, and protective order status, with options to register for status-change notifications.

The chapter includes a caution: absence of online criminal records does not mean no crime was committed. These tools are described as secondary to direct inquiry through courts or law enforcement, and failure to find online information about a known crime is common.

## Regional Records and the IntelTechniques Tool

Once a target location is established, searching for publicly available databases specific to that city, county, or state often reveals sources not found through national aggregators. Business and licensing records typically sit with the Secretary of State's office, property tax records with the county assessor, and jail and public assistance records at the county level. Cities hold licensing, permitting, and municipal court records.

The IntelTechniques Business and Government Tool (inteltechniques.com/tools/Business.html) provides a single interface that replicates several of the searches described in this chapter.
