## Overview

This unit is the introductory section for the nine-chapter block on leaks, breaches, logs, and ransomware. It establishes the legal framing, the investigative rationale, and the author's position before the technical chapters begin.

## Legal Warning

The techniques in this section are for educational use only. Executing them may violate organizational policy. Possession of publicly available stolen data could also violate a security clearance or be determined illegal by state or federal law. Distribution of such data may be illegal in most cases. The analogy offered is viewing stolen Hillary Clinton emails posted on WikiLeaks, or a stolen Google document posted on a blog. The author is not an attorney and offers no legal advice. Readers are told to verify with their organization's legal counsel or supervisory personnel before taking any action. The warning is explicit: accessing another person's account without a valid search warrant or court order is never acceptable.

## What the Data Is

The section focuses on large collections of stolen database content that circulate publicly on the internet. Services such as Have I Been Pwned (HIBP) hold partial copies of this data and allow email-address lookups to check for known compromises. The author's premise is that building your own unredacted, offline copy provides more investigative value than relying on those limited lookup services.

## Investigative Justification

The author argues that criminals have been using and sharing these techniques for years, and that investigators who ignore the data are at a disadvantage. The choice framed is between ignoring the data and confronting it directly to become a better investigator. The section promises that valid email addresses and passwords for billions of accounts can be accessed, and that using such data as an investigation technique is distinct from attempting to log into someone's account, which remains prohibited.

## Tone and Scope

The nine chapters that follow expand on what the previous (tenth) edition covered in a single section. The author acknowledges the content is controversial and encourages readers to form their own opinions, including disagreement, as a sign of genuine engagement with the material.
