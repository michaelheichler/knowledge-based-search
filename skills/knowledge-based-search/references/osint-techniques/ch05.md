## Overview

The Introduction establishes what the book is, who it is for, how it should be used, and the author's rationale for publishing methods that could theoretically be misused.

## What OSINT is

Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) is formally defined as intelligence produced from publicly available information that is collected, exploited, and disseminated in a timely manner to an appropriate audience for the purpose of addressing a specific intelligence requirement. The meaning varies by audience: for the CIA it may mean foreign news broadcasts. For an attorney, it may mean public government documents. For most readers, it is publicly available content found on the internet.

## Origins and context

The author taught his first OSINT course in 1999 to a group of local police chiefs in Illinois, before the term OSINT was widely used. The field has since expanded well beyond law enforcement into hacking conferences, cybersecurity strategy, journalism, and social engineering. Training audiences have grown and now include people with minimal technical experience who are assigned to investigation or analyst roles.

## What the book contains

The book provides several hundred free, publicly accessible information sources and software tools that can surface personal information about a subject under investigation. All resources are free and open to the public, with a few minor exceptions. Each method is explained, creative search techniques are detailed, and case examples are included where relevant. Digital companion files, terminal commands, and scripts are hosted at https://inteltechniques.com/osintbook11 (username: osint11, password: bk3t7). The online resource is treated as a vital extension of the book because commands change over time and the printed text can become outdated.

## Scope and limits

Finding information online is not the final step. The book covers collection and reporting: investigators must document findings and preserve content immediately, because websites shut down and data is removed without warning. The book does not discuss illegal methods. It is not a historical survey of OSINT, a policy discussion, or a guide to identity theft.

## Intended audiences

The book addresses multiple overlapping audiences:

- Law enforcement officers investigating missing children, human trafficking, or cold cases.
- Intelligence analysts handling social media.
- Private sector security teams monitoring physical threats or counterfeit products.
- Human resources departments and background-check personnel.
- Private investigators who need effective methods without deep technical expertise.
- Parents and teachers seeking to locate content posted online by children.
- University students (several universities have adopted the book as required reading in network security courses).

## How to use the book

The book is organized as a chronological guide but is designed to be used as a reference. Readers can skip chapters that feel too technical and return to them later. The Linux chapter (Chapter 2) is flagged as a common sticking point for beginners. Readers who find it overwhelming are encouraged to move ahead and return when ready. Every skill level is accommodated.

## Rationale for publishing sensitive techniques

The author acknowledges that some readers may use the methods for harmful purposes. His reasoning for publishing anyway: anyone determined to misuse this information can already find it through independent research. The benefit of equipping legitimate users (investigators, analysts, parents, security professionals) outweighs the risk of enabling a small number of bad actors.
