title: Eleventh Edition Preface
source: OSINT Techniques (Bazzell, Edison, 11th ed.)
---

This unit is the preface to the eleventh edition, written in late 2024 and accurate as of December 1, 2024.

The tenth edition was written in late 2022. Two years later the authors were asked to update it, primarily because the book is required reading for college courses, university degree programs, and government training academies. The authors estimate that 20% of the content is brand new, 20% has been updated to reflect changes through 2024, and 60% is carried over from the tenth edition. The eleventh edition also incorporates the full text of two previously separate digital supplement guides, "The Ultimate Virtual Machine" and "Leaks, Breaches, and Logs," replacing the tenth edition content on those topics. The result is described as a "Complete Works" of OSINT material relevant for 2025. Outdated content from the tenth edition was removed.

Two primary goals shape this edition. The first is self-reliance. The edition introduces a completely rebuilt Debian Linux OSINT virtual machine that is simpler to create, more stable, and more private than the earlier Ubuntu builds. The authors provide all commands needed to replicate their system and offer an automated build script. The second goal is updated OSINT technique coverage. Many search methods targeting Facebook, YouTube, X (formerly Twitter), Instagram, and other platforms that appeared in earlier editions have stopped working as those companies changed their products. The new edition offers replacement solutions.

The most significant structural change is the addition of co-author Jason Edison. Edison runs the official IntelTechniques online video training program and conducts live training sessions worldwide. Michael Bazzell explains that he no longer personally conducts OSINT investigations as part of his daily work, while Edison works actively for a government agency in an OSINT capacity. To keep a consistent voice, both authors write in the first person ("I," "me," "my") throughout the book as a collective presentation, and both stand behind the entire guide.

A technical note addresses editing. A book of this size (over 300,000 words) normally goes through five rounds of editing, but this edition received only two. The reduced editing was partly driven by hard course deadlines and partly by the problem of book piracy: shortly after prior editions were released, the majority of readers were downloading illegal PDF copies rather than purchasing the book. Readers who find remaining typos are invited to report them to books@inteltechniques.com. Print and digital PDF versions are available at https://inteltechniques.com/books.html.

The preface closes with a standing caveat: websites change or disappear constantly, and techniques that work at publication may fail later. Readers are encouraged to apply the broader strategic lessons from the book to adapt when specific methods break.
