---
title: Creating and Maintaining Covert Accounts for OSINT Investigations
source: OSINT Techniques (Bazzell, Edison, 11th ed.)
---

Chapter 23 covers the creation and maintenance of covert online accounts, called "sock puppets" by some practitioners. Many social networks now require a login before allowing any search queries, so an investigator who uses a personal account risks exposing their identity to a target through friend recommendations, accidental clicks, or platform notifications. The chapter argues that personal accounts should never be used for investigations and that investigators should maintain multiple dedicated covert profiles.

## Email for Covert Accounts

Every social network requires an email address at registration. The chapter recommends against using major providers such as Gmail, Yahoo, MSN, or Proton Mail because these are heavily associated with spam and receive more scrutiny from platforms. The preferred provider is Fastmail (ref.fm/u14547153). Fastmail is recommended for two reasons: it does not require a pre-existing email address to sign up, which breaks any link back to personal accounts, and it is less monitored by services like Facebook. Anyone can open a free 30-day trial account. The chapter suggests choosing an address ending in fastmail.us rather than fastmail.com because that domain is less common. After 30 days the free trial terminates, so investigators running long-term operations should use a paid account, which provides up to 600 permanent alias addresses.

## Platform-Specific Account Creation

**Facebook** is the most difficult platform. It often demands a cellular phone number for verification and rejects VOIP numbers such as Google Voice. The recommended approach is to disable any VPN or Tor Browser, clear browser cache, log out of all existing accounts, and connect from a residential, business, or cellular internet connection. Under these conditions the phone number requirement is often bypassed. If account creation still fails, the platform has flagged the browser fingerprint or IP address, and the investigator must start over with a fresh browser and a different network connection. Public library Wi-Fi is listed as the best option. Instagram follows the same rules because it is owned by Facebook.

**X (Twitter)** requires a covert account because many third-party investigative tools also require a logged-in X session. A legitimate email address used from a residential or business connection is normally sufficient. VPNs sometimes work during X account creation, but not reliably.

**Google/Gmail/Voice** has become more aggressive at rejecting suspicious registrations but remains achievable. Google blocks accounts created over Tor or VPNs. Providing a Fastmail address as a secondary contact usually satisfies Google's validation requirement. Google also appears more permissive when accessed through Chrome rather than a privacy-configured Firefox browser.

## Network Considerations

The chapter recommends conducting investigations behind a VPN but warns that creating accounts through a VPN often triggers suspicion. The advised workflow is to create the account from open public Wi-Fi (a library or coffee shop), then wait one day before switching to VPN access. From that point, the investigator should always use the same VPN provider and approximate location for that account. This consistency builds a recognizable usage pattern, which helps prevent the platform from locking the account.

## Phone Number Verification

When a platform flags a new account as suspicious, it demands a valid cellular number. Landlines and VOIP numbers are rejected. The chapter describes using Mint Mobile eSIM activations, available for $0.99 each on Amazon, as the practical solution. Each activation provides a real cellular number with a one-week free trial. The process is to activate the eSIM on an old Android phone, use that number to complete account registration across platforms, then immediately replace the number in account settings with a VOIP number and enable two-factor authentication.

## Securing and Maintaining Accounts

Once an account is active the investigator should immediately enable all available two-factor authentication options (a text message or a software token such as Authy). Activating 2FA signals to the platform that a real person is operating the account rather than an automated bot. The investigator must also access the account at least weekly. Accounts that sit dormant for months are likely to be suspended on the next login.

## Building a Credible Profile

An empty profile may be enough for some investigations, but empty accounts are more likely to be suspended by Facebook and may alert a target who runs their own OSINT check on the investigator. The chapter recommends populating covert profiles with fake but consistent details, using no information connected to the investigator's real identity.

For profile images, the recommended tool is This Person Does Not Exist (thispersondoesnotexist.com), which uses AI to generate photorealistic faces of people who do not exist. The images do not appear elsewhere online and can be downloaded in bulk for future use.

For identity details (name, birthdate, birthplace, username, password, zodiac sign, religion, political affiliation), the recommended tools are ElfQrin (elfqrin.com/fakeid.php) and Fake Name Generator (fakenamegenerator.com). The chapter includes a sample generated identity as illustration: Ella Theresa Bowman, born November 7, 1998, in Walnut Creek, CA, username boe431.

For a fake physical residence, This Rental Does Not Exist (thisrentaldoesnotexist.com) generates AI images of home interiors that can be posted as home photos. For timeline content and friends on Facebook, joining public pop culture or hobby groups causes the platform to automatically associate the covert account with other group members, supplying a continuous source of connections and content. Posting a resume online tied to the alias identity adds another layer of credibility if a target investigates the covert profile.

The chapter closes by noting that personal accounts carry the risk of accidental contact with a target (one errant click can send a friend request) and that some platforms, especially Facebook, will eventually surface the investigator to the target as a suggested friend. For these reasons, covert accounts should always be used in place of personal ones.
