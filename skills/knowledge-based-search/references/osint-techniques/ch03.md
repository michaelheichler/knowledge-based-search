This unit is the "About the Authors" section that precedes the main text. It introduces the two authors.

**Michael Bazzell** spent over 20 years investigating computer crimes for the U.S. government. For most of that time he was assigned to the FBI's Cyber Crimes Task Force, where he conducted online investigations and Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) collection. He also served as a sworn federal officer through the U.S. Marshals Service, and his casework spanned online child solicitation, child abduction, kidnapping, cold-case homicide, terrorist threats, and advanced computer intrusions. Over that career he trained thousands of individuals in his investigative techniques and privacy control strategies. After leaving government service he acted as the technical advisor for the first season of the television hacker drama "Mr. Robot." His two books, "OSINT Techniques" and "Extreme Privacy," are used by government and private organizations as training manuals for intelligence gathering and privacy hardening. He now works with individual clients on privacy protection, both proactively and in response to an undesired situation, through IntelTechniques.com.

**Jason Edison** is a 25-year veteran of a major U.S. metropolitan police department, where he serves as technical lead for the agency's Criminal Intelligence and Cyber-Crime Sections. He instructs internal department teams and outside organizations in Operational Security for Covert Operations, Countering Electronic Surveillance, Cyber-Crime Investigations, and Open-Source Intelligence. Before his current role he worked as a Federal Task-Force Officer conducting undercover and electronic surveillance operations for long-term organized crime investigations.
