This chapter (labeled Chapter 45 in the text) lays out a complete, repeatable workflow for OSINT investigations, from receiving an assignment through cleanup and handoff. The central argument is that staying organized and following a consistent process matters as much as knowing the individual tools. The chapter is addressed to both new practitioners and experienced investigators who want to refine an existing process.

## Receiving the Mission (Intake)

The first step is intake: accepting and understanding the assignment. Common OSINT request types are threat assessments for individuals (who is threatening, where are they, what is their capability and intent), threat assessments for events (who is involved, what are their intentions, what is the scale of impact), target profiles for individuals (full online presence including email addresses, home addresses, friends, hobbies), target profiles for organizations (online footprint and technological infrastructure), subscriber identification and account attribution (identify the person behind a domain, IP address, or account), and vulnerability assessments (map sensitive data exposures and operational security weaknesses for an individual or organization).

## Triage

Triage is deliberate planning before any searching begins. Its purpose is to prevent wasted effort from rushing in without direction. Depending on urgency, triage might take 30 seconds or 30 minutes.

The first action is articulating the mission objectives as explicit questions, preferably in writing. A concrete example from the chapter: "To be clear, you want to know the real name and physical addresses associated with the person in control of the email account of ramit@iwillteachyoutoberich.com, and we have two hours to accomplish this. Is this correct?" The written confirmation creates clear expectations and catches errors such as confusing the victim's identifiers with the suspect's, which the chapter notes happens frequently.

From that confirmation, the investigator lists primary goals explicitly. For the example above those would be: find the legal name of the real person associated with ramit@iwillteachyoutoberich.com, and find any home and work addresses.

During triage the investigator should also ask about the source and reliability of initial leads (information without context is not intelligence), gather any available context about the target (location, profession, known associates, culture), and consider whether OSINT is the right tool at all. A two-minute phone call to a postal inspector can sometimes identify the occupants of a residence faster than hours of online searching.

Additional triage actions:

**Legal preservation letters.** In law enforcement contexts, if a platform such as Gmail may hold relevant data, issue a preservation letter to retain it in anticipation of a future subpoena or search warrant. The ISP contact list at https://www.search.org/resources/isp-list/ is the recommended starting point for finding the correct contact at a provider.

**Deconfliction.** Check with colleagues in other units or agencies to confirm no parallel investigation is already underway. The chapter cites a case where two teams unknowingly ran separate operations in the same criminal community, wasting time by trying to gain reputation with users who were actually other undercover investigators.

**Note-taking setup.** Start a paper scratch pad for quick captures during browsing, and open a digital notebook such as Microsoft OneNote for pasting content. Create a new notebook section titled with the date, incident type, or case number, for example "OSINT Request Jan_10_2025" or "Robbery 25-486544." The chapter flags that using Microsoft products involves data collection and that this should be cleared against the organization's operational security requirements.

## Knolling Your Tools

"Knolling" is organizing the workspace before beginning actual searches. The analogy used is a surgeon's instruments sanitized and laid out before an operation. Recommended preparation steps:

- Verify VPN connection and consider connecting to a server in the target's region.
- Start the OSINT virtual machine and run any updates.
- Open the paper notepad, digital notebook, and any investigation template.
- Log into covert accounts in the investigative browser.
- Open the OSINT tools dashboard at https://inteltechniques.com/tools/ and pin the tab.
- Create a logically structured case directory and subdirectories on the workstation or an external drive.

## Closed-Source and Premium Data

The research phase begins with in-house or paid sources before touching free OSINT tools. These return leads quickly with minimal effort. Recommended sources:

- Commercial aggregators: Accurint (LexisNexis), TLO, Clear. These draw heavily on credit histories and utilities, making them strong for residential address history, landline phone numbers, employers, roommates, and family members. They work poorly for email addresses, usernames, and social media accounts.
- Premium people-search services: BeenVerified, Intelius, Spokeo, Pipl, WhitepagesPro. Subscription costs range from roughly $15 to $300 per month. Everything from paid aggregators is available for free elsewhere, but with considerably more time.
- Government and law enforcement databases: Department of Licensing, criminal records, Department of Corrections, agency records management systems. These often include photos of the target, which can be used to verify or rule out possible social media accounts.

Promising identifiers from this phase go into case notes. Generated reports are saved as PDFs into the digital notebook. Photos are pasted into notes or dropped into the case directory.

## Open-Source Research and Collection

With premium sources exhausted, the investigator moves to free OSINT tools. The OSINT tools dashboard is the primary hub. The chapter recommends adding the OneTab extension (one-tab.com) to Chrome and Firefox within the VM.

Tab management is the organizing principle. The rule is to exhaust each tab completely before moving on, never clicking into interesting links before finishing the current page. Opening a new lead prematurely is called "rabbit holing" and is described as one of the most common mistakes made by new investigators.

Practical tab workflow:

1. Open the custom OSINT tools using the category matching known identifiers, such as the email or search engine tools.
2. Review Google results in order. Right-click promising links and choose "Open link in new tab."
3. Always open the image results for the query. Image results allow fast visual scanning.
4. Move between tabs with Ctrl+Tab (Windows) or Command+Tab (Mac). Add Shift to reverse direction.
5. Close tabs that are false positives immediately to reduce clutter and workstation load.
6. When a page yields a cluster of strong leads that each require their own full query set, open each in a new browser window rather than a new tab. Each strong lead becomes an independent branch.
7. Write new account identifiers on the paper notepad. Create a dedicated page in OneNote for each strong lead. Mark a lead page as complete by adding a "+" symbol or other character to its title.

OneNote structure: a section per investigation, with pages for a scratch pad (quick paste of links and reminders), request details, premium and government data reports, and one page per strong lead. The notebook title reflects the case number or target name.

**Context menu queries.** The ContextSearch extension (github.com/ssborbis/ContextSearch-web-ext) allows right-clicking any highlighted term or link to query it against a configurable list of search engines and custom URL patterns. Custom searches are added through the extension settings under the Search Engines tab by pasting a query URL and appending {SEARCHTERMS} at the end. This speeds up workflow but does not replace the full custom toolset.

## Tab Management with OneTab

When finished with a set of tabs, right-click anywhere in the window, click the blue OneTab icon, and choose "Send all tabs to OneTab." This collapses all tabs into a saved list. Add a logical title such as "Google Target Name" by clicking in front of the tab count. To find the set later, press Ctrl+F (Windows) or Command+F (Mac) and search the custom title.

To move the URL list into the digital notebook: click "Export/Import URLs" in OneTab, select and copy the URL set, then paste it into the appropriate notebook page.

Alternative tab managers for online sync or advanced features: Toby, Tabs-Outliner, Workona, Graphitabs. The chapter notes that extensions always carry a security cost and should be used sparingly.

## Capture and Collection

Three approaches to preserving evidence:

**Manual capture.** The investigator directly saves pages and images. Folder structure: a case folder in the VM's shared directory, with subfolders matching tool categories (for example, "Email Addresses") and then subfolders for each specific identifier (for example, the email address being queried, named exactly). Every saved image or video should be accompanied by a full-page screenshot of the page from which it was taken, stored in the same folder, to show its visual context.

**Passive capture with Hunchly.** Hunchly is a Chrome extension that records all pages loaded in Chrome at the source code level, including images, creating a forensically sound capture log automatically. Steps: create a Hunchly case matching the notebook and directory name, confirm the extension is active and set to the correct case name, then proceed with normal research. Key images are tagged by right-clicking and choosing the Hunchly context menu entry, adding a caption, and saving. Hunchly can generate a forensically sound report of all tagged images.

**Scripted capture** is noted as a third approach (the chapter text as printed reproduces the Hunchly description again for this section, which appears to be a duplication in the source text).

## Multimedia and Link Analysis

Multimedia analysis means examining recovered images and video both visually and for metadata. Visual examination proceeds at the highest available resolution, looking for incidental details such as business signage in the background of a profile photo that narrow down identity or location. The chapter describes this as time-consuming but one of the best methods for locating a target who has otherwise covered their online tracks.

Link analysis uses charts and diagrams to show connections between entities: people, locations, websites, phone numbers, and account identifiers. The recommended free tool is Draw.io (draw.io), available as both a desktop application and a browser-based editor. Not every case requires a link chart, but it is useful for complex organizations or any case that benefits from a visual representation of how accounts and entities connect.

## Submission and Cleanup

Before moving to the next case:

- Transfer handwritten notes to the digital notebook or final report. Scan paper notes as PDF if preferred. Shred or file paper notes per agency policy.
- Archive the case directory in compliance with evidence retention policies. The chapter recommends retaining working files on network-attached storage or optical discs, because historical notes save significant time if a subject resurfaces.
- Export a snapshot of the VM used for the case if appropriate, then revert to a clean snapshot. Prepare fresh covert accounts and replace any broken tools for the next investigation.

## Time-Compressed Workflow (20 Minutes vs. 20 Days)

The same workflow applies to long investigations and to rapid threat assessments, with time per step reduced for urgent work. The quality of work and operational security both degrade when time is compressed.

An example from the chapter is a user threatening suicide in an online chatroom. The scenario given: "User D1ckTraC on 4chan is threatening to kill himself in a post. We want to know who he is, where he lives, and whether he is likely to carry out the threat."

Streamlined steps for this scenario:

1. Verbally clarify known identifiers and expected intelligence output.
2. Grab a legal pad and pen. Open the OSINT VM. If fresh accounts are not available, use utility accounts (pre-staged social media accounts held in reserve for time-critical situations). Fresh accounts are always preferred.
3. Run the target through commercial aggregators and government databases quickly.
4. Begin with Google using a targeted query such as site:4chan.org "username". Then query known identifiers through the custom OSINT tools.
5. Open only the most promising links. Scan pages visually, prioritizing image results because they can be processed faster than text.
6. Note anything useful on the legal pad and leave the corresponding tab open.
7. Be ready to deliver a situational report at the deadline even if identification is incomplete. Example briefing: "We have located and preserved the original posting, there is a history of similar suicidal threats from that user, but we do not know who or where he or she is yet."
8. Complete collection, analysis, and documentation after the crisis passes, then fall back into the normal workflow.

For team collaboration during a rapid assessment: use a shared OneNote notebook with each person pasting findings to their own page. Use the browser-based version of OneNote for near-instant sync (the desktop version has multi-user sync issues). Assign one person to track overall progress and build the big picture.

## Target Flowcharts

The chapter addresses a question common in OSINT training: is there a standard process for each type of target? The answer is that no template covers every scenario, but directional standards can be developed. Six flowcharts are provided for the most common starting identifiers: Email, Username, Real Name, Telephone Number, Domain Name, and Location.

The principle behind each is to move from one identifier type toward the next. An email address leads toward usernames and real names. A username leads toward social networks and email verification. A real name leads toward email addresses, usernames, and a telephone number. A telephone number leads toward a verified name, physical address, and relatives. A domain name leads toward a real name and physical address. Each newly discovered identifier restarts the cycle.

All flowcharts are available for download at https://inteltechniques.com/data/osintbook11/flowcharts.zip as both image exports and an editable Draw.io source file.
