"The Kit" is an introductory resource from the Exposing the Invisible project at Tactical Tech. It frames investigation as a form of civic engagement and presents itself as a starting point for anyone who treats information as evidence.

## What the Kit is and why it exists

The Kit opens by acknowledging that investigative work is often slow, demanding, and sometimes dangerous, yet it argues that this process can turn a curious person into a skilled documentarian, researcher, or activist. The team at Exposing the Invisible has spent years promoting investigation as one of the most consequential forms of engagement, not just political engagement but any deliberate effort to participate in public life. Their stated goal is to help people develop three core abilities: questioning information that is false, finding information when it is scarce, and filtering information when it becomes overwhelming. The team positions these skills as a way to counter the suppression of opinions, bypass censorship, combat excessive secrecy, and increase transparency so that people can rely on facts rather than on opinions and assumptions.

From the start the project has focused on expanding the skills people already have, including techniques people have developed from their own devices and the digital or physical spaces they inhabit. The Kit explicitly does not restrict itself to digital tools or data-driven methods. The authors state that their trust is placed "not in technology or data but in information and knowledge."

## Who the Kit is for

The Kit describes itself as meant for everyone. Prior skills and experience are not prerequisites. It is open equally to someone considering their first investigation and to someone who has conducted many. The only requirement is curiosity and a willingness to ask questions.

The Kit is also described as issue-agnostic. It is not limited to investigations of corruption or abuses of power. The authors hold that meaningful change happens everywhere and at every scale, and that even challenging one's own assumptions is a legitimate reason to use the resource. The Kit is presented as a place to refine thinking and approach work in a thorough, step-by-step way.

Ethically, the Kit promotes safe and responsible methods for seeking answers, gathering evidence, and surfacing new narratives. The authors encourage what they call "seeing sideways," a phrase they use in Exposing the Invisible to mean curiosity and creative thinking. They acknowledge that patience is required and make no claim that the work is easy. Turning information into evidence, then into knowledge, and then into wisdom or justice demands time, effort, persistence, and courage. The authors argue these transformations are necessary for anyone who wants to remain engaged, active, and aware.

## Who made it

Tactical Tech launched Exposing the Invisible nine years before the Kit's publication with the goal of showcasing investigations carried out outside of formal journalism and law enforcement. Over that period the team produced documentary films, recorded interviews, analyzed investigative tools and tactics, and ran trainings and workshops. Two larger events preceded the Kit directly: a Data Investigation Camp in 2017 and a "Kit" Residency in 2018, which served as the primary starting point for the resource.

The Kit was published in April 2019. The team members named are Chris, Laura, Marek, and Wael. The Kit is described as a work in progress, with additional content to be released over time. Feedback and collaboration inquiries can be sent to eti@tacticaltech.org.
